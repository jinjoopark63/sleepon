## What would flip a no

- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I fall asleep 10+ minutes faster or stop waking up from the sound cutting off, and it came as a one-time payment of about 10,000 won instead of a subscription.
- If the 14-day comparison actually showed I fall asleep a lot faster than with my usual 30-minute timer, I'd think about the yearly plan, but only at around 2,000 won a month.
- If the day-14 comparison actually showed I fell back asleep after the 3-4am wake noticeably faster, like 15+ minutes, I'd pay the yearly price once.
- If the 14-day comparison showed I fell asleep clearly faster, like 15+ minutes faster, I'd pay the yearly 39,000 won, and only if there were a student discount.
- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I fall asleep 15+ minutes faster with it, and there was a student discount around 1,500-2,000 won a month.
- If the day-14 comparison clearly showed I fell asleep at least 10 minutes faster on the SLEEP:ON nights, I might pay for one month to see if it holds up.
- If the 14-day comparison showed I fell asleep a lot faster, like 15+ minutes, and there was a cheap student price around 2,000 won a month or a one-time purchase.
- If the 14-day comparison showed me falling asleep clearly faster, like 10+ minutes, on the SLEEP:ON nights with my AirPods on.
- If my own 14-day result showed a big, obvious drop in how long it takes me to get back to sleep after waking at 3-4am, and the app store reviews said its sleep detection is accurate, I'd pay the 39,000 won yearly plan.
- If the 14-day report actually showed I fell asleep clearly faster (like 10+ minutes) on the SLEEP:ON nights, I'd pay the yearly 39,000 won.
- If the 14-day comparison actually showed me falling asleep 10+ minutes faster with my own data, I'd consider the yearly plan, but only if it were closer to 1,000-2,000 won a month.
- Solid app-store ratings or an independent test showing the phone's sleep-onset detection matches a watch or sleep tracker, and then my own 14-day result showing a clear difference.
