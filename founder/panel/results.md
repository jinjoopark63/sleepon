## What would flip a no

- If after the free 14 days it showed me I really fall asleep a lot faster, like 15+ minutes, and the 3am resume never once woke me up, I'd think about the yearly plan.
- If the free 14-day comparison showed I actually fall asleep noticeably faster, like 10+ minutes, with the resume feature off and nothing ever waking me up, I might pay the yearly price.
- If the 14-day report showed I got back to sleep noticeably faster after waking at night, not just faster the first time I fell asleep, I'd consider the yearly plan.
- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I fall asleep 10+ minutes faster and the yearly plan were closer to 20,000 won, I'd pay once for the year.
- If the 14-day comparison showed a big gap, like 20+ minutes faster, and a cheap one-time price or a student price around 2,000 won a month.
- If on the 14th day my own records showed I fell asleep noticeably faster (around 10 minutes or more) on SLEEP:ON nights, I might pay for the annual plan at 39,000 won.
- If the 14-day comparison actually showed me falling asleep 15+ minutes faster on the SLEEP:ON nights, I'd consider the yearly plan, but only if it were closer to 20,000 won.
- If the 14-day trial showed I actually fell asleep a lot faster, like more than 10 minutes, I might think about it, but only if it cost about as much as a coffee for the whole year.
- If the 14-day report also showed my night wake-ups and how fast I got back to sleep with the resume feature vs without, and it clearly helped, I'd pay the yearly plan.
- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I fall asleep something like 15 minutes faster with the SLEEP:ON mode, I'd think about the yearly plan, as long as it's around 2,000 won a month.
- Seeing the 14-day result match my smartwatch data with a clear, consistent difference, plus solid app-store reviews from people who say the measurement was accurate.
- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I got back to sleep a lot faster after the 3-4am wake-ups, I'd consider the 39,000원 yearly plan.
