# Buyer panel: SLEEP:ON (스르르), a Korean sleep-sound app

**7 buy · 13 pass** (35% buy) out of 20 simulated buyers. Seed 1126, so the same cards can be dealt again.

These are simulated buyers, not customers. Use this to find objections and weak spots, then confirm the big ones with real people before you spend.

## Why they pass

| reason | buyers | in their words |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| habit | 8 | "YouTube sleep music with a timer already costs me nothing, and I've never paid for a sleep app. I might try the free 14 days since there's no auto-charge, but after a long overtime week I won't pay 4,900 won a month for something I barely notice once I'm asleep." (P001) · "No auto-charge on the trial is the one thing that gets me to install it, so I'd run the 14 days for free. But I already fall asleep to free YouTube ASMR with my AirPods, so unless the comparison shows a big difference I'll just stay on the free version like I do with every app." (P003) |
| trust | 4 | "I'd probably start the free 14-day trial since there's no auto-charge, but I wouldn't pay. It's a new app with no ratings I can check, and I don't believe a phone on the mattress can tell when I fell asleep or woke up well enough to make the day-14 comparison mean anything." (P004) · "I'd probably run the free 14 days since there's no auto-charge and the daytime-sleep part actually fits my mornings after nights, but I wouldn't pay off the back of numbers measured by a phone lying on the mattress. I already fall asleep to free sounds on YouTube, so the app has to prove itself to me first." (P009) |
| price | 1 | "I'd probably start the free 14-day comparison since there's no auto-charge, and fading volume with earphones in actually suits my lab hours. But I make almost nothing, and YouTube sleep music already works fine for me, so I don't see myself paying 4,900 won a month or 39,000 won up front on top of the subscriptions I already have." (P016) |

## Why they buy

| reason | buyers | in their words |

| need | 7 | "After overtime I lie there 30-40 minutes, and timer apps cut out right when I'm drifting off, so a fade that follows when I put the phone down actually fits my messy schedule. The trial costs nothing and doesn't auto-charge, so I'd try it, and if the 14-day comparison shows I fall asleep faster I'd pay the 4,900 won monthly, not the yearly one." (P002) · "My bedtime moves every day, so a clock-based 30-minute timer never fits me, and the 14-day trial costs nothing and doesn't auto-charge. If the side-by-side numbers show I actually fall asleep faster, 4,900 a month is less than a coffee, so I'd pay. I'd likely start monthly before trusting it with the yearly plan." (P006) |

## What would flip a no

- If the 14-day comparison actually showed I fell back asleep clearly faster after waking at night, I'd consider the yearly plan, since that's my real problem.
- If on day 14 my own records showed I fell asleep clearly faster, like 10+ minutes sooner on the SLEEP:ON nights, I'd pay the monthly 4,900 for a month to see if it holds.
- If my own 14-day comparison showed a clear drop in how long it takes me to get back to sleep, and the app had solid app-store reviews from other shift workers saying the auto-resume doesn't fire by mistake.
- If the day-14 comparison showed a real gap, like falling asleep 15+ minutes faster after night shifts, I'd cancel my current app and switch to this instead.
- If my own 14-day comparison showed a clear gap, and it matched what a watch recorded on the same days (or there were solid app-store reviews from other shift workers saying the same), I'd pay the yearly 39,000.
- If after the 14 days my own records showed a big, consistent gap, like falling asleep 15+ minutes faster on SLEEP:ON nights, I might pay for the yearly plan once.
- If the comparison showed I got back to sleep a lot faster after waking at night, like 15+ minutes, on most nights, I'd pay the monthly 4,900 for a month to check.
- If the 3-day dry run showed it would only have restarted when I was actually awake, and the day-14 comparison showed I fell asleep clearly faster on SLEEP:ON nights, I'd pay the 3,250-a-month yearly plan.
- If the day-14 comparison showed I got back to sleep clearly faster after waking up at night, like 10+ minutes faster, I'd pay the 4,900 for one month to see if it holds up.
- If the 14-day report showed a big, obvious gap, like falling asleep 15+ minutes faster or getting back to sleep much quicker after waking at night, and it cost around 2,000 won a month or there was a student price.
- Lots of real app-store reviews from shift workers, or some outside check showing the phone's sleep times match a watch or a sleep study, plus my own 14 days showing a clear difference, not a few minutes.
- If the 14-day comparison clearly showed I fell asleep faster after night shifts on the SLEEP:ON days, I'd cancel my current app and switch to the 39,000 won yearly plan.
